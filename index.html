What is an ARD meeting?

In Texas, ARD means Admission, Review, and Dismissal — the team that decides eligibility and builds the IEP.

Admission

Reviews evaluation data and decides if the student qualifies.

Review

At least yearly: progress on goals, services, and placement.

Dismissal

When services are no longer needed or the student exits.

Who must be on the committee?

  • You — full equal member
  • Regular and special education teachers (as applicable)
  • District representative and evaluation interpreter
  • Your child when appropriate

Your core rights

From IDEA and Texas Education Code Chapter 29.

  • Prior written noticeAt least 5 school days ahead unless you agree in writing to meet sooner.
  • Full participationYou are a voting member. Your concerns must be considered.
  • Access to recordsRequest educational records in writing before the meeting.
  • Bring supportAdvocate, attorney, or friend — no school permission needed.
  • IEEIf you disagree with the school’s evaluation, request an IEE at public expense in writing.
  • Disagree and documentRefuse to sign, note disagreement, or take time to review.
  • Record (Texas)Audio-record with at least 24 hours written notice.

Safeguards & timelines

Evaluation request

Generally 15 school days to respond after a written FIIE request.

ARD notice

At least 5 school days written notice.

Recording notice

At least 24 hours if you plan to audio-record.

Annual review

IEP reviewed at least once a year.

Prior Written Notice (PWN)

Required when the school proposes or refuses changes to identification, evaluation, placement, or FAPE.

Red flags

Vague language

“Support” or “monitor” without measurable goals or minutes.

No data

“Progressing well” without numbers or samples.

Pressure to sign now

You may take the IEP home to review.

Not in writing

Verbal promises not in the IEP are not enforceable as part of it.

Questions to ask

  • How is my child progressing on each goal?Ask for measurable data.
  • Are goals measurable?Condition, skill, and criteria.
  • What services, minutes, frequency, location?Get it written into the IEP.
  • Is this the least restrictive environment?Start with general education plus supports.

Letter templates

Copy, fill in, send, keep a copy.

Request for records

Subject: Request for educational records – [Child’s Name]

Dear [Coordinator],
I request a complete copy of educational records for [Child’s Name], including evaluations, IEPs, progress reports, and discipline records.
Thank you,
[Your Name] | [Phone] | [Email]

Request for ARD

Subject: Parent request for ARD – [Child’s Name]

Dear [Coordinator],
I request an ARD for [Child’s Name] to discuss [reason]. Available: [dates]. Please give 5 school days’ written notice.
Thank you,
[Your Name]

Notice to record

Subject: Notice of intent to audio-record ARD – [Date]

This email gives at least 24 hours’ notice that I will audio-record the ARD for [Child’s Name] on [date] at [time].
[Your Name]

What to say

Opening — “I’d like to share my top priorities: [1–3 items].”
Vague progress — “Can you show the data for each goal?”
Services — “Please put type, minutes, frequency, and location in the IEP.”
Disagree — “I disagree with [decision] because [reason]. Please note my disagreement and provide PWN.”

When you disagree

Informal resolution

Another ARD or meeting with the special education director.

Mediation

Voluntary, confidential, free through the state.

State complaint

Written complaint to TEA.

Due process

Formal hearing. Consider legal advice.

Stay-put

While due process is pending, the student often remains in the current placement.
